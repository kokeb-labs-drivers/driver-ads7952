Hardware¶

The ADS7950, ADS7951, ADS7952 and ADS7953: pins, bus settings, the frame, the modes, the program registers and power, as the driver uses them. Values are from the datasheet; when your schematic disagrees on pin numbers, the schematic wins.

The family¶

Part Channels Resolution Supported
ADS7950, ADS7951, ADS7952, ADS7953 4, 8, 12, 16 12 bits Yes: Config::variant
ADS7954, ADS7955, ADS7956, ADS7957 4, 8, 12, 16 10 bits No
ADS7958, ADS7959, ADS7960, ADS7961 4, 8, 12, 16 8 bits No

All twelve share the frame, the modes and the registers and are pin compatible within a package size (section 5). The 10- and 8-bit parts put fewer result bits in DO11:00 and compare their alarm thresholds differently (Table 13); the driver handles the 12-bit members only. The class is named after the ADS7952, the part it was written for.

Bus settings¶

Setting Value
Mode 0: SDI latched on the rising edge, SDO changes on the falling edge (Figure 2)
fSCLK max 20 MHz at any +VBD (section 7.9)
tw1, CS high min 20 ns
Frame Exactly 16 clocks with CS low; CS may go high only after the 16th (section 8.1)
Transfer from the driver Always 2 bytes, one frame; CS must toggle between frames

The SCLK rate sets the timing of the analog side: the multiplexer switches on the second falling edge of a frame and the next channel acquires from the 14th rising edge to the next CS falling edge (section 8.1). A slow clock and gaps between frames give a high-impedance source more time to settle. The acquisition time is 325 ns minimum (section 7.6), and with MXO shorted to AINP the source impedance should be 50 Ω or less, since a higher one slows settling after a channel change (section 9.1.1). To check your input network, read the same inputs at your SCLK and at 1 MHz and compare the means: a difference of more than 1 to 2 LSB calls for a lower source impedance or a buffer between MXO and AINP (section 9.2.2).

The frame¶

Figure description and sources

Each column is one 16-bit frame, CS low for 16 SCLK cycles, left to right in time. In each panel the top row is the word the driver sends on SDI, the middle row the channel the multiplexer acquires during that frame, and the bottom row the reply on SDO: the conversion of the channel acquired in the frame before, sampled at this frame's CS falling edge, tagged with its address in DO15:12. In Manual mode (top) ReadChannel(4) selects channel 4 with a Manual word, sends a "continue" word and then the Manual word for channel 4 again (it carries the range once more), and keeps the third reply, whose address must be 4; the two replies before it still belong to the previous channel. In Auto-1 (bottom), entering the mode restarts the sequence at its first channel: with channels 2, 5 and 6 programmed, the first result arrives two frames after the Auto-1 word, and from then on every Auto-1 word without restart (DI11 = 1, the range again) returns the next channel of the sequence, so a pass costs one frame per channel. The timing is the datasheet's Figures 51 and 53; the frame counts come from ReadChannel(), readSelected(), scan() and collect() in src/ads7952.ipp.

Drawn from: src/ads7952.ipp select(); src/ads7952.ipp selectChecked(); src/ads7952.ipp readSelected(); src/ads7952.ipp scan(); inc/ads7952_registers.hpp kContinue; figure module docs/diagrams/figures/frame-pipeline.py.

Each frame is one 16-bit word in and one out, MSB first. The word in (DI15:00) has its command in DI15:12:

DI15:12 Word Driver use
0000 Continue in the current mode; DI11:00 ignored (Table 7) Every frame that only reads
0001 Manual mode, next channel in DI10:07 (Table 1) ReadChannel(), ReadChannels(), the identity check
0010, 0011 Auto-1, Auto-2 mode (Tables 2, 5) ReadAuto1(), ReadAuto2()
0100 GPIO program register (Table 11) Start-up reset, GpioConfig
1000 Auto-1 program: the mask follows in the next frame (Table 3) SetAuto1Channels()
1001 Auto-2 program: last channel in DI09:06 (Table 6) SetAuto2LastChannel()
11bb Alarm program, group bb; thresholds follow (Table 13) SetAlarmThresholds()

In a mode word, DI11 = 1 makes the chip take DI06:00 (DI10:00 in the Auto modes); with DI11 = 0 it keeps the previous values. DI10 restarts an Auto sequence; DI06 is the range; DI05 powers down; DI04 makes the next reply carry the GPIO levels instead of the channel address; DI03:00 are the GPIO output levels. The driver always sends DI11 = 1 with the configured range and levels, and DI04 = DI05 = 0 except where a call needs them.

The word out has the channel address in DO15:12 and the 12-bit result, straight binary, in DO11:00 (reg::ReplyAddress(), reg::ReplyCode()). It is the conversion of the input sampled at that frame's CS falling edge, which the multiplexer acquired during the frame before, which the frame before that chose: a channel chosen in frame N comes back in frame N + 2 (Figure 51). A range written in frame N applies from the same result. The first conversion after power-up or a reset is invalid (section 7.6).

Modes¶

Mode Channels Frames per result
Manual The one each word names 3 for a new channel, then 1 for each repeat
Auto-1 The channels of a 16-bit mask, ascending, wrapping 1, after 2 to enter
Auto-2 Channel 0 up to a last channel, wrapping 1, after 2 to enter

Entering an Auto mode from another mode restarts its sequence (sections 8.4.5 and 8.4.6). Writing the Auto-1 mask while in Auto-1 moves the chip on to the next enabled channel of the new mask; writing the Auto-2 last channel while in Auto-2 restarts it at channel 0. The chip keeps converting in its mode while a program register is written, and its replies stay valid.

Program registers and their reset values¶

Register Reset value Config field
Mode control Manual, channel 0, Range 1, GPIO outputs low range, gpio.output_levels
Auto-1 mask Every channel (0x000F, 0x00FF, 0x0FFF, 0xFFFF) auto1_channels
Auto-2 last channel The last channel (3, 7, 11, 15) auto2_last_channel
GPIO Every pin an input, no function gpio
Alarm thresholds High all ones, low all zeros: no alarm alarms

DI09 of the GPIO program word resets every register "in the next CS frame" (Table 11): EnsureInitialized() uses it as the chip's software reset, then writes every register. The chip has no way to read a register back.

Alarms¶

Each channel has a high and a low threshold of 10 bits, compared with the upper 10 bits of each result (Table 13): the two lowest bits of a code do not count. GPIO0 can be the "high or low" or the high alarm output and GPIO1 the low alarm output (TSSOP only), active high, set on the 12th SCLK of the frame that converts the channel and cleared on the 10th SCLK of the next (section 8.1). Programming a group of four channels takes the group frame, one frame per threshold and one frame to leave the sequence.

Power¶

Item Value
+VA 2.7 V to 5.25 V
+VBD 1.7 V to +VA
VREF 2.0 V to 3.0 V, 2.5 V nominal; lower than 2.4 V costs noise (section 7.6)
Range 2 0 to 2 x VREF, only while 2 x VREF <= +VA (section 7.6): Validate() checks it with vref_mv and va_min_mv, the lowest +VA the board guarantees
Inputs -0.3 V to +VA + 0.3 V, absolute (section 7.1)
Decoupling 1 µF ceramic at each supply pin; 10 µF ceramic from REFP to REFM, next to the pins (sections 8.3.1, 10)
Power-down DI05 = 1: down at the end of the next frame (section 8.3.2); about 1 µA
Power-up 1 µs, then one invalid conversion (section 7.6)

Choosing VREF and the range for a supply, the scale monitor, ratiometric sensors and the layout rules: Reference, range and supplies.

Deinitialize() powers the chip down. EnsureInitialized() wakes it through the reset, waits kPowerUpUs with the port's DelayUs() and discards the invalid conversion.
